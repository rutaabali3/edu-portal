Key Learning Features

Observe

Use maps, stories, data, and local observations to describe what is changing and where.

Explain

Connect greenhouse effect, water, energy, ecosystems, and human decisions without losing nuance.

Act and evaluate

Choose a practical action, define who benefits, and revisit the result rather than treating action as a one-time gesture.

Evidence and Models

Compare observations, indicators, and modeled projections; record assumptions and uncertainty before drawing conclusions.

Citizen Science

Use documented projects such as iNaturalist or local programs to collect observations, check identifications, and record limitations.

Educational Videos

Use NASA, NOAA, WWF, and National Geographic videos and interactives as starting points, then follow the linked evidence and context.

Explore NOAA Climate Data

Classroom-ready climate data and Earth science

NOAA’s educator collection connects learners with classroom-ready modules, historical and real-time climate data, maps, satellite imagery, graphs, and modeled projections. Use it to practice asking questions of evidence rather than treating a visualization as a prediction by itself.

  • Classroom-ready climate data activities
  • Historical and real-time observations
  • Maps, graphs, satellite imagery, and models
  • Guided activities for data literacy
  • Audience and access vary by resource
Open NOAA Climate Data

NOAA resources include classroom-ready activities and accessible data tools; individual datasets, projections, and external resources have their own documentation and scope.
