The learning loop

Represent, explore, explain

Tools are most useful when they help you see structure. Start with a question, choose a representation, vary one thing at a time, and write the reasoning in words.

01

Represent

Translate a situation into numbers, a diagram, a table, an equation, or a graph.

02

Explore

Use sliders, constructions, and examples to look for invariants, patterns, and counterexamples.

Investigate
03

Explain

State assumptions, show steps, check units and edge cases, and explain why the answer follows.

Practical answers

Questions beginners ask

It depends on the task and rules. As a learning tool, a solver can help you check or compare methods, but you should be able to state the input, explain the method, and reproduce the reasoning when required.

A graph can reveal intercepts, roots, transformations, rates of change, domains, and behavior that is difficult to see symbolically. It complements algebra rather than replacing it.

Spend a few minutes retrieving foundations, solve one unfamiliar problem, review one mistake, and explain one idea in your own words. Consistency beats a long session once a week.
